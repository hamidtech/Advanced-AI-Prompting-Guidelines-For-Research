# Religion and theology research profile

[فارسی](theology.fa.md) · Version 2.0.0 · Updated 2026-10-31

## Questions and contribution

Begin by distinguishing confessional theology, jurisprudential or doctrinal argument, historical study of religion, comparative religion, philosophy of religion, and empirical study of religious communities. These approaches can interact but do not share every criterion of evidence or validity.

A question about what a tradition ought to hold differs from one about what a historical text says, how interpreters have read it, or what present-day participants report. A theological claim is not verified by counting textual matches. Participant agreement does not establish doctrinal authority. Conversely, a doctrinal argument is not evidence of actual population behavior.

A contribution may clarify a concept, reconstruct an argument, compare a defined interpretive dispute, establish a textual relation, or document lived practice. Do not insist that every project discover an empirical “gap” or develop an automated classifier.

## Materials, constraints, and sources

Possible materials include scripture, commentaries, legal texts, creeds, sermons, manuscripts, ritual records, interviews, and material culture. Establish tradition, school, language, historical period, genre, edition, attribution, and the relevant criteria of authority. Preserve differences between primary scripture, commentary, translation, later synthesis, and contemporary opinion.

Use verified primary editions and specialist scholarship appropriate to the question. Check provenance in an institutional catalogue or the edition itself; a web quotation without a traceable edition is a search lead. When citing scripture, supply the tradition's stable reference system and the translation/edition used. When citing commentary or legal argument, supply author, work, edition, and a locator. Do not invent a verse, hadith, chain of transmission, legal ruling, authenticity grade, or consensus claim. A model is not a religious authority.

Access to archives, language proficiency, interpretive training, community trust, participant consent, and local ethical requirements can determine feasibility. Respect restrictions on sacred or community-controlled materials. Critique arguments without attributing motives to believers or treating a tradition as internally uniform.

## Method and verification

For textual work, define the interpretive method and distinguish a source's explicit statement from your reconstruction. For legal or doctrinal work, declare the school and permitted inferential rules; do not silently combine incompatible assumptions. For historical work, evaluate attribution and chronology rather than assuming traditional attribution settles every historical question.

For empirical community research, justify sampling and consent, preserve anonymity, and avoid treating a small interview sample as representative of all believers. For digital work, account for orthographic variants, diacritics, OCR, quotation, intertextuality, and edition differences. A repeated phrase is a candidate connection requiring contextual interpretation, not proof of borrowing or agreement.

## Adapt the generic prompts

Supply the research stance, tradition/school, primary corpus, languages, period, authority criteria, access, and intended contribution. If the stance is undecided, ask the model to clarify it before drafting a final title. Require alternative interpretations and their source warrants. Label outputs as textual observation, historical hypothesis, normative argument, empirical finding, or AI inference. Any claim of consensus needs a defined scope and verified supporting scholarship.

## Completed example: a bounded commentary comparison

**Illustrative design; no passage, doctrinal conclusion, or consensus is asserted.**

Completed prompt:

> I am a Master's student in religious studies with one semester, Arabic reading skills, and access to two verified editions of commentaries from a specified Islamic interpretive tradition. I have not yet selected the passage or supplied edition records. I want a comparative textual study of how a concept of justice is explained. Use conservative exploration. Ask me to specify the tradition, commentaries, passage, and research stance before proposing final titles. Distinguish my comparative analysis from a religious ruling. Do not invent quotations, authenticity judgments, or consensus. Offer a rival explanation for any apparent difference.

After the student supplies verified records, a bounded candidate title could be **“Explanatory uses of the concept of justice in two selected commentaries: a contextual comparison of a defined passage.”** This is a title pattern, not identification of actual works. The contribution may be a clarified interpretive distinction, pending specialist literature review.

The plan records the passage reference, edition details, page/volume locators, relevant surrounding text, and the terms each author uses. A comparison distinguishes explicit definitions from inferred premises and later scholarly commentary. One rival explanation is that apparent disagreement arises from genre or audience rather than doctrine. The researcher checks both readings against the original text and relevant specialist arguments.

If the editions are based on different textual witnesses, evaluate whether this affects the comparison. If language access is inadequate, narrow the scope or seek qualified supervision rather than relying on AI translation. If the project shifts to contemporary believers, redesign the evidence collection and ethics process; textual analysis cannot answer that empirical question by itself.

**Decision:** conditionally feasible for a textual comparison, subject to corpus access and supervision. **Next validation step:** verify editions and passage scope, then search for prior comparisons and contrary interpretations. **Evidence status:** research design only; no theological verdict or verified novelty.

## Review before proceeding

Is the research stance explicit? Are authority criteria and source levels distinguished? Are quotations verifiable? Are minority interpretations and disagreement represented fairly? Does the conclusion remain within the chosen textual, normative, historical, or empirical scope?
