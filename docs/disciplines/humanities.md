# Literature and humanities research profile

[فارسی](humanities.fa.md) · Version 2.0.0 · Updated 2026-10-31

## Questions and contribution

Research may ask how a concept changes, how a text constructs meaning, how editions differ, how an argument works, or how a work is situated historically. Close reading, archival research, textual criticism, intellectual history, philosophical argument, and digital analysis support different kinds of contribution.

A contribution can be a defensible interpretation, a clarified concept, a critical edition, a newly connected archive, a translation argument, or a critique of an established reading. Do not require a quantitative hypothesis, statistical significance, or “prediction accuracy” for an interpretive project. Originality is assessed against relevant scholarship and argumentative adequacy, not against a model's claim that an idea sounds new.

## Materials, constraints, and sources

Materials may include manuscripts, editions, translations, letters, images, performances, oral histories, and critical scholarship. Distinguish the historical work, the material witness, the edited text, the translation, and a digitized transcription. Record edition, editor, publication details, page/folio, language, and access restrictions.

Consult primary materials and critical editions, specialist bibliographies, archival catalogues, and original scholarly arguments. Search catalogues in the relevant languages; translated keywords can miss conceptual distinctions. A digitized text may omit apparatus, layout, marginalia, or variants. For digital transcription planning, the [TEI Guidelines on representing primary sources](https://vault.tei-c.org/P5/4.2.1/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/PH.html) describe ways to represent facsimiles and source features. This is a versioned reference, not a requirement that every project use TEI. Accessed 2026-10-31.

## Method and verification

Declare the interpretive approach and justify corpus boundaries. Use close reading to test computationally surfaced patterns. Frequency does not establish meaning, influence, intentionality, or reception. A stylometric or topic-model result needs a research question, appropriate comparison, and checks for edition, OCR, genre, and period effects.

For philosophical work, reconstruct arguments charitably, distinguish premises from conclusions, and address strong objections. For historical interpretation, avoid anachronism and preserve contextual uncertainty. Translations by AI are working aids; check consequential terms against the original and qualified scholarship. Never invent quotations, page numbers, manuscript variants, or archival shelfmarks.

## Adapt the generic prompts

Add the corpus, editions, original languages, time range, interpretive tradition, access limits, and the kind of contribution sought. Request alternative readings with textual warrants, including evidence that weakens the preferred interpretation. Permit unresolved ambiguity. Ask for search leads when material is unavailable rather than an invented reconstruction. Use “argumentative and textual adequacy” instead of a generic feasibility score where appropriate.

## Completed example: comparing a metaphor across editions

**Illustrative design; no quotation, source identification, or literary finding is asserted.**

Completed prompt:

> I am a Master's comparative-literature student with 14 weeks. I can read Persian and French and have legal access to one Persian poetry collection and two French translations, but I have not supplied bibliographic records or excerpts yet. I want to examine how metaphors of distance are translated. Propose a bounded close-reading design. Use balanced exploration. Ask for edition information before identifying passages. Do not invent quotations or assume one translation is more faithful. Separate descriptive comparison from interpretation and propose a serious rival reading.

A candidate question is **“How do two French translations render metaphors of distance in a selected Persian poetry corpus?”** The scope is a small, justified set of passages, not the poet's entire worldview. The potential contribution is an argued account of translation choices relative to existing scholarship; it remains unverified until the bibliography is searched.

The student first supplies verified edition records and selected passages with page numbers. A comparison table retains the Persian text, each French rendering, a literal working gloss, translator notes where present, and unresolved lexical choices. Close readings examine imagery, syntax, sound, and local context. One rival explanation is that a difference reflects idiomatic constraints rather than a different interpretation of distance.

If one translation is abridged or uses a different source edition, compare only aligned passages or make that difference part of the question. OCR text is checked against page images. A model may suggest candidate patterns but cannot supply absent lines or certify translation quality. No numerical ranking of translators is required.

**Decision:** provisionally feasible if editions and enough comparable passages are accessible. **Next validation step:** verify edition provenance and closely read two aligned passages before fixing the corpus. **Evidence status:** proposed interpretive study; no finding claimed.

## Review before proceeding

Are quotations and editions traceable? Are alternative interpretations treated fairly? Do computational claims exceed their textual basis? Is ambiguity retained where the source warrants it? Does the contribution fit the chosen scholarly tradition?
