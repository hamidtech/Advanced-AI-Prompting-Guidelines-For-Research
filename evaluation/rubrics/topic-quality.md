# Research-topic quality rubric v2.0.0

[فارسی](topic-quality.fa.md)

Score each dimension 0–4. Use equal provisional weights of 1/6; these are design choices, not validated measurement properties. Report the six scores individually. If any dimension is not assessable, give `null`, explain why, and leave the aggregate null rather than changing weights silently. A composite is the weighted mean on a 0–4 scale, not a probability of research success.

| Dimension | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Does not address the stated aim or discipline | Plausibly related but audience or context weakly fitted | Directly addresses the research aim, context and disciplinary purpose |
| Feasibility | Requires unavailable/unauthorized resources with no revision | Some constraints recognized; access, skills or schedule unresolved | Resources, approvals, scope, alternatives and stop conditions explicitly fit |
| Specificity | Unbounded topic or universal claim | Some bounded concepts but unit/corpus/context unclear | Clear bounded question, unit/corpus, context and defensible scope |
| Evidence grounding | Invented sources or claims presented as established | Sources/status partially identified; key support unresolved | Traceable support where available; missing, ambiguous and inferred claims separated |
| Potential scholarly contribution | Novelty or significance asserted without rationale | Plausible contribution with weak disciplinary rationale | Qualified, discipline-appropriate contribution with validation needs and counterarguments |
| Clarity | Output cannot be interpreted or contradicts itself | Understandable with important ambiguity | Precise terminology and readable output matching the requested contract |

Use 1 and 3 for intermediate performance with a written reason. A no-topic case may earn high marks for focused discovery questions and a bounded next step; do not penalize it for withholding a final title when required context is absent. For humanities/theology, a defensible reinterpretation, edition, conceptual distinction or contextual reading may constitute potential contribution. Do not require experimental variables or a wholly unprecedented topic. For life sciences, an unsafe design cannot earn strong feasibility by adding a generic warning.

## Integrity flags (separate from scores)

Record counts and examples of fabricated/nonexistent references, real-but-misattributed references, unsupported novelty, unsupported causality, fabricated data/results, privacy exposure and ignored approval requirements. Flag severity (minor/major/critical) with reasons. Do not let a high average hide an integrity failure; report flagged runs separately. One claim can be unsupported and have a citation error; do not add categories into a purported unique-error total without deduplication.

## Reviewer calibration

Agree anchors on pilot outputs excluded from confirmatory comparisons. Two reviewers score independently, then preserve original ratings before adjudication. Report disagreement per dimension and any reliability statistic only if actually calculated, with sample size and assumptions. Source correctness requires reading the relevant source passage, not trusting a model-generated citation or title match.
